How to Calculate a Cleaning Hourly Rate

A cleaning hourly rate calculator helps cleaning professionals estimate how much to charge for each billable hour. A sustainable rate should account for labor, cleaning supplies, transportation, insurance, equipment, administration, and the profit the business aims to earn.

Hourly Rate Formula

Estimated cost per hour = labor cost + supplies cost + other hourly expenses + allocated monthly expenses.

Suggested hourly rate = estimated cost per hour ÷ (1 − desired profit margin).

For example, if the total cost is 20 currency units per hour and the desired profit margin is 20%, the suggested rate is 20 ÷ 0.80 = 25 currency units per hour.

What Expenses Should You Include?

  • Employee wages or your own labor cost.
  • Cleaning products and consumable supplies.
  • Travel, vehicle, and transportation expenses.
  • Insurance, licences, and business registration.
  • Equipment replacement and maintenance.
  • Scheduling, administration, and marketing expenses.

Hourly Rate vs. Profit

Revenue is the money received from customers. Profit is the amount remaining after business costs. A higher hourly rate does not automatically guarantee more profit: compare your prices with your costs, service quality, local market, and customer requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge per hour for cleaning?

The appropriate rate depends on your location, labor costs, service type, overhead, competition, and target profit margin. Use your actual costs to estimate a suitable rate.

What is a billable hour?

A billable hour is time you can charge to a customer. Time spent on administration, marketing, travel, or other unpaid work may not count as billable time.

What is profit margin?

Profit margin is the percentage of revenue remaining as profit after the costs included in your calculation.

Can I change the currency?

Yes. Choose USD, INR, GBP, EUR, CAD, or AUD. The selector changes the displayed symbol only; it does not convert exchange rates.

Does this calculator guarantee my profit?

No. This is an estimate based on your inputs. Actual results depend on customer demand, billable hours, expenses, taxes, and other factors.
