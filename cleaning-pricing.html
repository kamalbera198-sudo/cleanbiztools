How to Calculate a Cleaning Price

A cleaning price calculator turns the size of the job, the type of service and your own costs into a price you can quote with confidence. This one works in five steps. Window cleaning, travel, tax and the deposit are optional.

Step 1: Calculate the base price

Base Price = (Area × Rate per sq ft) + (Bathrooms × Charge) + (Bedrooms × Charge) + (Kitchens × Charge) + (Other Rooms × Charge)

Step 2: Adjust for service, dirt level and frequency, then add extras

Cleaning Price = (Base Price × Service Factor × Dirt Factor + Add-Ons) × (1 − Frequency Discount)

Step 3: Add window cleaning and travel (optional)

Window Price = (Standard + Large × 2 + Small × 0.75) × Rate × Method Factor × Height Factor × Dirt Factor + (Total Windows × Extra Charges per Window)
Work-Based Price = Cleaning Price + Window Price + Travel Charge

Window cleaning is priced as its own line, so the cleaning service factor and the recurring-visit discount do not change it. The travel charge is a flat fee added after everything else.

Step 4: Calculate the cost-based price

Cost-Based Price = Total Job Costs ÷ (1 − Target Margin)

A 30% target margin means dividing your costs by 0.70. That is different from adding 30% to your costs, which would give you a lower margin than you planned.

Step 5: Add tax and a deposit (optional)

Total with Tax = Suggested Price × (1 + Tax Rate)
Deposit = Total × Deposit % (or a fixed amount), and Balance = Total − Deposit

Tax is added on top of your suggested price. Profit and margin are always calculated before tax, because tax is not income for your business.

Pricing tip: the calculator compares your work-based price, your cost-based price and your minimum job charge, then suggests the highest of the three. If the work-based price is already high, your actual margin will be above your target. The Actual Profit Margin and Minimum Price boxes show where you stand. Your real profit can be lower if you have costs that are not entered here.

Cleaning Price Example

A customer wants a deep clean of a 1,500 sq ft home with 2 bathrooms. The home is quite dirty, so you choose heavy dirt. The customer also wants the inside of the oven cleaned. Your rate is 0.12 per sq ft, you charge 10 per bathroom and 25 for the oven. Labor costs 150 and supplies cost 20.

  • Area charge: 1,500 × 0.12 = 180
  • Bathroom charge: 2 × 10 = 20
  • Base price: 180 + 20 = 200
  • After service and dirt factors: 200 × 1.5 × 1.2 = 360
  • Work-based price with the oven: 360 + 25 = 385
  • Total job costs: 150 + 20 = 170
  • Cost-based price at a 30% margin: 170 ÷ 0.70 = 242.86
  • Suggested price: 385 (the higher of the two)
  • Estimated profit before other expenses: 385 − 170 = 215
  • Actual margin: 215 ÷ 385 = 55.8%

Window cleaning example

A two-story home has 12 standard windows, 2 large windows and 4 small windows. The customer wants interior and exterior cleaning with normal dirt. Your rate is 5 per standard window and you charge 1 per window for tracks and sills.

  • Weighted windows: 12 + (2 × 2) + (4 × 0.75) = 19
  • Base window price: 19 × 5 = 95
  • After method and height factors: 95 × 1.8 × 1.25 = 213.75
  • Tracks and sills: 18 windows × 1 = 18
  • Window price: 213.75 + 18 = 231.75

Travel, tax and deposit example

Take the house cleaning example above, where the work-based price is 385 and job costs are 170. The customer lives far away, so you add a travel charge of 15. Sales tax is 8% and you ask for a 25% deposit.

  • Work-based price with travel: 385 + 15 = 400
  • Suggested price before tax: 400 (above the cost-based price of 242.86)
  • Profit before tax: 400 − 170 = 230, a 57.5% margin
  • Tax at 8%: 400 × 0.08 = 32
  • Total with tax: 400 + 32 = 432
  • Deposit due now at 25%: 432 × 0.25 = 108
  • Balance after the deposit: 432 − 108 = 324

These are worked examples, not recommended prices for every market. Use your own rates and costs.

What Affects Cleaning Prices?

  • The size of the property and the number of bedrooms, bathrooms and kitchens.
  • The type of service: regular, deep or post-construction.
  • How dirty the property is and how long it has been since the last clean.
  • Extras such as ovens, fridges, pet hair and window cleaning.
  • The number, size and height of the windows.
  • Travel distance and the time it takes to reach the job.
  • Sales tax, VAT or GST where it applies.
  • Labor, travel time, supplies and equipment.
  • Insurance, overheads and other business costs.
  • How often the customer books you.
  • Local competition and what customers expect to pay.

Pricing Tips for Cleaning Businesses

  • Set a minimum job charge so short jobs still cover travel and setup time.
  • Time every job and compare what you earn per hour with your target.
  • List exactly what is included in each quote so there are no surprises.
  • Charge extra for heavy dirt, pet hair and difficult access.
  • Reward regular customers with a discount, but only if the price still covers your costs.
  • Ask for a deposit on large or first-time jobs, so a cancelled booking does not cost you the time you set aside.
  • Print the estimate or save it as a PDF to keep a record of how you reached each quote.
  • Review your real profit after each job and update your rates when costs rise.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge for house cleaning?

Your price depends on the size of the property, the type of service, how dirty it is, your labor and supply costs, travel and your local market. Use this calculator to estimate a price, then compare it with what other cleaners charge in your area.

How do I calculate a cleaning price?

Multiply the area by your rate per square foot, add a charge for each bathroom, adjust for service type and dirt level, add any extras, then apply a discount for recurring visits. Compare the result with your cost-based price and use the higher number.

Should I charge per square foot or per hour?

Per square foot gives customers a clear, predictable quote for standard homes. Hourly pricing suits unusual or hard-to-estimate jobs. Whichever you use, check that the final price covers your hourly earnings target.

How much more does a deep clean cost than a regular clean?

A deep clean takes more time than a regular clean. This calculator uses an adjustable multiplier of 1.5 times for a deep clean and 2 times for a post-construction clean. These are assumptions, so change your rate to suit your market.

Can I use this calculator outside the USA?

Yes. Choose USD, CAD, GBP, EUR, AUD or INR. The currency selection only changes the displayed symbol and does not convert exchange rates, so enter all amounts in your own currency. You can also enter square metres and a per-square-metre rate.

Does this calculator include window cleaning?

Yes. Tick the window cleaning option, then enter how many standard, large and small windows there are, your price per standard window, the cleaning method, the building height and the dirt level. You can also add per-window charges for tracks and screens. For a window-only quote you can also use the Window Cleaning Price Calculator.

Can I add tax and a travel charge to the estimate?

Yes, both are optional. Enter a travel charge to add a flat trip fee to the price. Enter a tax rate such as sales tax, VAT or GST to see the tax amount and the total with tax. Profit and margin are always calculated before tax.

How does the deposit work?

Choose a percentage or a fixed amount and enter the value. The calculator shows the deposit due now and the balance left after the deposit, based on the total with tax when a tax rate is entered.

Can I print the estimate?

Yes. Press the Print estimate button to print the result, or choose Save as PDF in the print window of your browser.
