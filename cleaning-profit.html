How to Calculate Cleaning Business Profit

A cleaning business profit calculator helps business owners understand how much money remains after operating expenses. It can help you review job costs, compare periods, and make informed pricing decisions.

Net Profit Formula

Net Profit = Total Revenue − Total Expenses

Profit Margin Formula

Profit Margin (%) = Net Profit ÷ Total Revenue × 100

Expense Ratio Formula

Expense Ratio (%) = Total Expenses ÷ Total Revenue × 100

For example, if your cleaning business earns $250 and spends $125, the estimated profit is $125 and the profit margin is 50%.

Common Cleaning Business Expenses

  • Labor: Employee wages and cleaning labor costs.
  • Supplies: Cleaning products, cloths, gloves, and bags.
  • Travel: Fuel, parking, and job-related travel.
  • Equipment: Vacuum cleaners and cleaning tools.
  • Insurance: Relevant business insurance costs.
  • Marketing: Advertising and customer acquisition.
  • Administration: Software, phone bills, and office costs.

Include the expenses that apply to your business. Avoid counting the same expense more than once. If you do the cleaning yourself, include a wage for your own time so your profit figure is realistic.

Tips for Improving Cleaning Business Profit

  • Track revenue and expenses consistently.
  • Review how much time each cleaning job takes.
  • Include labor and supplies when setting prices.
  • Review recurring expenses and identify unnecessary costs.
  • Compare profit across jobs to understand which services perform best.

A higher revenue figure does not always mean higher profit. Monitor expenses as well as sales when evaluating business performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a cleaning business profit calculator?

It estimates net profit by subtracting the expenses you enter from your total revenue. It also shows your profit margin and expense ratio.

How do I calculate cleaning business profit?

Add up all your expenses, then subtract them from your total revenue. Net profit = total revenue minus total expenses. Use the same time period for both numbers.

How do I calculate my profit margin?

Subtract expenses from revenue, divide the result by revenue, and multiply by 100. If revenue is zero, the calculator displays 0%.

How do I calculate profit per cleaning job?

Enter the revenue for the job and the costs for that job only: labor, supplies, travel and any other direct costs. The net profit and margin shown are for that one job.

What is a good profit margin for a cleaning business?

There is no single right number. It depends on your location, services, pricing and whether you use employees. A useful approach is to compare the margin of each job and each month, then focus on the jobs and periods that fall below your own average.

What is the difference between profit margin and markup?

Margin is profit divided by revenue. Markup is profit divided by cost. For example, if a job costs $125 and you charge $250, your profit is $125. That is a 50% margin but a 100% markup.

Which expenses should I include?

Include labor, supplies, travel, equipment, insurance, marketing, software and any other business costs that apply. If you do the cleaning yourself, include a wage for your own time, otherwise your profit will look higher than it really is.

Can I use this calculator for one cleaning job?

Yes. Enter the revenue and expenses for that job to estimate its profit. You can also use it for a week, a month or a full year.

Does the calculator calculate taxes?

No. It estimates profit based only on the revenue and expenses entered. Tax treatment depends on your circumstances and location.
