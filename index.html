House Cleaning Pricing Guide

Setting cleaning prices requires an understanding of home size, service requirements, labor costs, supplies, travel, and other business expenses. This free house cleaning pricing calculator helps you organize these factors into a starting estimate.

1. Consider the Size of the Home

A larger property may require more work than a smaller property, but square footage alone does not determine the final price. Home condition, bathrooms, kitchens, pets, clutter, and customer requests can also affect cleaning time.

2. Choose the Right Cleaning Service

Standard cleaning, deep cleaning, and move-in or move-out cleaning may require different amounts of time and effort. The service factors in this calculator are editable examples in concept, but their preset values are not universal industry rates.

3. Include Labor Costs

Consider the number of cleaners, expected work hours, and your actual labor expense. If you employ workers, account for applicable payroll costs and other employment expenses when preparing your full business budget.

4. Include Supplies and Travel

Cleaning products, equipment, fuel, parking, and travel time may affect job profitability. Include relevant costs when preparing a customer estimate.

5. Understand Profit Margin

Profit margin is the portion of the selling price that remains as profit after the costs included in the calculation. It is different from adding a percentage markup to costs. This calculator uses the target profit margin to estimate a price based on entered costs.

Cleaning Pricing Formula

This calculator first estimates a base price using square footage and a selected service factor. It then compares that price with the price required to cover entered costs and the target profit margin. The higher of these two estimates is displayed as the suggested starting price.
