📘 How to Calculate Window Cleaning Prices

A window cleaning cost calculator helps you estimate a fair price based on the number of windows, window size, service type, building height, dirt level, add-ons and job costs.

Step 1: Calculate the Base Price

Base Price = (Standard Windows × Rate) + (Large Windows × Rate × 2) + (Small Windows × Rate × 0.75)

Step 2: Adjust for Service, Height and Dirt, Then Add Extras

Work-Based Price = (Base Price × Service Factor × Height Factor × Dirt Factor) + Add-Ons

Step 3: Calculate the Cost-Based Price

Cost-Based Price = Total Job Costs ÷ (1 − Target Margin)

For example, a 30% target margin means dividing the total job costs by 0.70. This is different from simply adding 30% to costs.

💡 Pricing tip: The calculator compares your work-based price with your cost-based price and selects the higher value. If your work-based price is already high, your actual margin will be above your target and changing the target will not change the price. The "Actual Profit Margin" and "Minimum Price" boxes show you where you stand. Your real profit may differ if you have additional expenses that are not included.

📝 Window Cleaning Price Example

Suppose you have a two-story home with 20 standard windows and 2 large windows. The customer wants interior and exterior cleaning. Your exterior rate is 5 currency units per standard window, labor costs 90 and supplies cost 10. Dirt level is normal and no add-ons are selected.

  • Standard windows: 20 × 5 = 100
  • Large windows: 2 × 5 × 2 = 20
  • Base price: 100 + 20 = 120
  • Work-based price: 120 × 1.8 × 1.25 = 270
  • Total job costs: 90 + 10 = 100
  • Cost-based price at a 30% margin: 100 ÷ 0.70 = 142.86
  • Suggested price: 270 currency units
  • Estimated profit before other expenses: 270 − 100 = 170
  • Actual margin: 170 ÷ 270 = 63.0%

This is a mathematical example rather than a recommended price for every market. Use your own rates and costs for a more relevant estimate.

🏠 Residential vs Commercial Window Cleaning

Residential Window Cleaning

Residential window cleaning usually involves houses, apartments and other homes. Pricing depends on window count, size, accessibility, interior or exterior service and additional work such as screens or tracks.

Commercial Window Cleaning

Commercial window cleaning may involve offices, shops, restaurants and larger buildings. Consider scheduling, access, equipment, travel, contract frequency and applicable safety requirements before setting your price.

For recurring contracts, calculate the price for one visit first. Then multiply it by the number of visits, accounting for any agreed discounts and changes in the scope of work.

🔎 What Affects Window Cleaning Prices?

  • Number of windows and their dimensions.
  • Whether interior, exterior or both sides are cleaned.
  • Building height and accessibility.
  • Heavy dirt, paint residue, stickers or hard-water marks.
  • Whether screens, frames and window tracks are included.
  • Labor, travel time, supplies and equipment expenses.
  • Insurance, overheads and other business costs.
  • Local competition and customer expectations.
  • Minimum job charges and recurring service arrangements.

💼 Window Cleaning Pricing Tips for Small Businesses

  • Set a minimum job charge to cover travel and setup time.
  • Track the time spent on each job and compare earnings with your hourly target.
  • Include appropriate charges for difficult access and additional work.
  • Clearly describe the services included in every customer quote.
  • Account for insurance, equipment, transportation and other overhead expenses.
  • Review actual profit after each job and adjust your rates when costs change.
  • For high-rise jobs, assess access and safety requirements before accepting the work.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge to clean windows?

Your price depends on the number and size of windows, service type, building height, labor, supplies, travel and local market rates. Use this calculator to estimate a price and compare it with your local market.

How do I calculate window cleaning prices?

Multiply your standard window rate by the number of windows, adjust for window sizes, service type, building height and dirt level, add any extras, and compare the result with your cost-based price.

Should I charge per window or per hour?

Per-window pricing is useful for predictable residential jobs. Hourly pricing can be useful for unusual jobs. Compare your quote with your hourly earnings target before accepting a job.

Does interior and exterior cleaning cost more?

Interior and exterior cleaning usually requires more work than exterior-only cleaning. This calculator uses an adjustable service factor to estimate the difference.

How do I calculate window cleaning profit?

Subtract labor and other job costs from the estimated selling price. Remember to include applicable overhead expenses when reviewing your actual business profit.

Can I use this window cleaning calculator internationally?

Yes. Select USD, CAD, GBP, EUR, AUD or INR. Currency selection changes the displayed symbol, not exchange rates. Enter all amounts in your selected currency.

Can this calculator estimate commercial window cleaning?

Yes. Enter the window counts, service type, building height, rate and costs. For commercial contracts, also consider access requirements, equipment, recurring visits and safety planning.

What affects window cleaning prices?

Window size, window count, cleaning method, building height, accessibility, dirt level, screens, tracks, labor, travel, equipment and local competition can all affect pricing.
